# Invariant Principles

1. Start from a measurable business problem, product vision, scope boundary, and success measure.
2. Requirements must be owned, prioritized, testable, and linked to the decisions or rules that justify them.
3. Match documentation and control weight to risk, compliance, uncertainty, and delivery cadence.
4. A completed increment is running, verified, integrated, and potentially releasable.
5. Keep model language, ownership, data boundaries, and integration contracts explicit.
6. Preserve change history, baseline identity, impact analysis, and acceptance evidence.
7. Use automated checks and refactoring to keep delivery speed from becoming quality debt.
8. Treat requirement uncertainty, domain ambiguity, and architecture unknowns as managed risks.
9. Use feedback from users, delivery, and operations to revise value, scope, risk, and priorities.

10. Architecture decisions must be driven by measurable quality scenarios and business change forces.
11. A view or pattern is justified by a stakeholder use or a concrete design problem, not by convention alone.
12. Interfaces document externally visible syntax, semantics, protocols, errors, timing, and evolution rules.
13. Architecture documentation must include rationale, mappings, assumptions, and known debt where they affect future decisions.
